The flavors Mireya grew up with.

Mireya Miller’s family is from Zacatecas, Mexico; she grew up in Kansas City falling for the restaurant business through her extended family’s kitchen back home. This menu is that inheritance, poured.

Why these drinks

“I always knew I wanted to have a coffee shop.”

“I’ve always known I loved coffee since I was a kid… I always knew I wanted to have a coffee shop.”— Mireya Miller, to the Kansas City Star, November 2026

A coffee cart turned corner shop, and the counter still does what a family counter does: sweet things done properly. The syrups that flavor the seasonal lineup are made in-house, and the shop is moving fully house-made, one batch at a time.

Fuego dulce

Habanero, meet dulce de leche.

The shop’s signature: a $7 latte that pairs habanero heat with caramelized milk. The Kansas City Star’s writer ordered it on opening week and filed the reassurance — if you’re hesitant about spice, don’t be; it’s a balance of sweet and a complementary heat kick.

The flavor list

Lechera. Dulce de leche. Horchata. Jamaica.

Lechera

Inspired by the sweet, milky cafés of Mexican home kitchens — dulce-sweet and smooth.

Dulce de leche

Caramelized milk, slow and golden — the base of the fuego dulce and a sweeten-everything move.

Horchata

The familiar cinnamon-rice refreshment, the way it’s poured at home.

Agua de jamaica

Hibiscus, cold and ruby-bright. Summer in a cup — even in November.
